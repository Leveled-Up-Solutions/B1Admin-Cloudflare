# Calendar and sermon APIs

These APIs are part of the selected Cloudflare milestone. They do not create the separate Potter's Hands website. Seeds contain synthetic development calendars and sermon descriptions only.

## Calendars and events

`/api/content/calendars` accepts GET and POST arrays. `/calendars/:id` accepts GET and DELETE. The calendar representation is `{id,churchId,name,description,color,timeZone,groupId,updatedAt}`. `groupId` may be null, `color` is six-digit hexadecimal, and `timeZone` must be an IANA zone. New records omit id; supplied ids update existing tenant records. A calendar containing events cannot be deleted or moved to another group until its events are moved or deleted.

`/api/content/events` accepts GET and POST arrays. `/events/:id` accepts GET and DELETE. `/events/group/:groupId` provides the upstream group-event read shape. Event fields are `{id,churchId,calendarId,groupId,title,description,start,end,allDay,location,timeZone,recurrenceRule,exceptionDates,updatedAt}`. Calendar is required; the event inherits its calendar's group and time zone unless supplied. A group calendar's event must use that same group. The calendar/group relationship is guarded in SQLite triggers as well as application validation so racing requests cannot bypass it.

Raw list reads accept `calendarId`, `groupId`, `start`, and `end` filters, with a maximum of 500 stored events. They return recurring series (including anchors outside the window) and overlapping standalone/replacement events; clients must expand recurring series rather than displaying only their anchors. Unfiltered results order by start time. Collections return arrays to retain the ChurchApps wire convention. For a display-ready calendar, use `/api/content/events/instances?calendarId=...&start=2027-03-01&end=2027-04-01`. This returns occurrences overlapping the start-inclusive/end-exclusive window, including events that began before it. Each occurrence includes a stable `occurrenceId`, `seriesId`, and civil `occurrenceDate`; base `id` remains the original event id. Standalone events retain their own id. The optional `recurrenceIndex` is the original series count position, before exclusions. Without bounds, instances default to today through 90 days ahead.

Timed events require an ISO timestamp with `Z` or an explicit numeric offset; they are normalized to UTC for storage. `timeZone` preserves the intended presentation zone. The offset determines the instant; the API does not infer DST offsets from a naive wall-clock string. All-day events instead require `YYYY-MM-DD` civil dates and retain them without converting to UTC. Their end date is exclusive: one all-day event on January 16 uses start `2027-01-16`, end `2027-01-17`. Empty, invalid, or reversed intervals are rejected. Date-only query windows are preferred when displaying a calendar that includes all-day events.

Staff access follows global or exact calendar permissions, and members use the separate `/api/member/v1/calendar` filtered surface. See [resource permissions](RESOURCE-PERMISSIONS.md) for scoped View/Edit, creation, move and delegation rules. Every relationship and query is scoped by church id. Batch writes and audit entries commit atomically. Overlapping events are permitted; these are calendars, not an exclusive room-booking system. Registration, rooms/resources, booking approval, holiday expansion, and external calendar synchronization remain unimplemented and are not simulated.

Named `/content/calendars` is an adaptation-specific API. Upstream organizes events through groups and curated calendars; basic `/content/events` fields and array writes are preserved, but complete curated-calendar interoperability is not claimed. Recurrence uses upstream `recurrenceRule` and `exceptionDates` fields and the `eventExceptions` route; invalid rules are rejected rather than silently falling back to a single event.

### Recurrence and occurrence edits

Rules use the upstream `rrule` 2.8.1 library ([BSD-3-Clause notice and python-dateutil attribution](LICENSE-RRULE)) with a validated subset: daily, weekly, monthly and yearly `FREQ`; `INTERVAL` 1–366; `BYDAY`, `BYMONTHDAY`, `BYMONTH`, `BYSETPOS`, `WKST`; and either `COUNT` 1–1000 or inclusive `UNTIL`. `UNTIL` accepts a civil `YYYYMMDD` or UTC `YYYYMMDDTHHmmssZ`. Embedded DTSTART/TZID, multiline ICS, hourly/minutely/secondly schedules, and unknown fields are rejected. Calendar UI follows the upstream daily/weekly/monthly patterns and also exposes yearly recurrence. Date-based endings stay civil rather than being converted using the browser's time zone.

Timed recurrences preserve the start and end wall-clock times in the event's IANA zone. Thus a New York 09:00 gathering changes UTC offset across DST. Nonexistent start/end times are skipped without consuming COUNT; repeated wall-clock times choose the earlier instant, while the supplied anchor retains its explicit offset. Events spanning a DST transition can change elapsed duration because local start/end times are preserved. All-day intervals remain civil dates with exclusive ends. Invalid month dates are skipped, as in the upstream RRULE library.

Expansion requires a positive window of at most 366 days within 1900–2200. Date-only bounds apply in the event's time zone; explicit timestamp bounds identify instants, including for all-day overlaps. Limits are 500 stored events, 1000 returned occurrences, 10,000 loaded exclusions, 20,000 generated candidates/estimated recurrence cycles, and at most 50 years between a series anchor and query end. Exceeding a limit returns 400 with a request to narrow the window/calendar or split an old series; results are never silently truncated. Member calendar reads apply tenant, calendar-group and event-group visibility before expansion, then paginate expanded occurrences.

Upstream-compatible POST arrays to `/api/content/eventExceptions` accept `{eventId,exceptionDate}`; dates may be civil or an offset timestamp that is converted to the series zone. Only dates that actually occur are accepted. GET `?eventId=...`, GET `/:id`, and DELETE `/:id` read or restore exclusions. Raw event DTOs expose sorted `exceptionDates`. Exceptions count against the original COUNT. Deleting an exception also removes its replacement, restoring the original occurrence atomically.

For atomic occurrence changes, GET `/api/content/events/:seriesId/occurrences/:YYYY-MM-DD` resolves the original occurrence even when excluded. POST to that route accepts one event patch, records the exclusion, and creates a standalone replacement in one D1 transaction; DELETE skips that date and removes any replacement. Replacements retain the series calendar/group and cannot themselves recur. Their `recurrenceParentId` and `recurrenceOriginalDate` link to the original series; clients should mutate them through the occurrence endpoint. Failed validation leaves both original and replacement unchanged.

POST `/api/content/events/:seriesId/future/:YYYY-MM-DD` accepts one event patch, truncates the old series before the selected occurrence, and creates a successor series atomically. Omitted COUNT rules keep the remaining occurrence count; supplying a rule explicitly changes the successor pattern/count. DELETE truncates without a successor. Future splits require `resetExceptions: true` when they would replace existing future exceptions; future deletion explicitly removes those exceptions. Editing an entire series through the regular POST array requires `resetExceptions: true` when scheduling fields change and exceptions exist. Title/description-only edits preserve them. Deleting a series cascades exceptions and replacements. The UI offers “This event only,” “This and future events,” and “Entire series,” plus restoration of skipped/edited dates.

## Sermon archive

`/api/content/sermons` supports GET and POST arrays; `/:id` supports GET and DELETE. Preserved fields are `title`, `description`, `speaker`, `publishDate`, `videoType`, `videoData`, `videoUrl`, `audioUrl`, `thumbnail`, `playlistId`, and `duration`. `/api/content/playlists` and `/:id` provide the corresponding playlist CRUD (`title`, `description`, `publishDate`, `thumbnail`). Playlists referenced by sermons cannot be deleted until sermons are moved or removed. Media URLs must be HTTPS. There are no YouTube/Vimeo imports, provider lookups, live streaming, or network requests during saves.

New sermons are private and unpublished. Set `visibility: "public"`, then POST `/api/content/sermons/:id/publish` to expose a sermon. DELETE `/:id/published` unpublishes. Publishing requires effective editing permission for that sermon, through a global, exact sermon or containing playlist grant. A published sermon whose publish date is in the future remains hidden. Edits to an already published public sermon become visible immediately; unpublish first when draft edits are needed.

The narrow future-website integration is GET/HEAD `/api/public/v1/churches/:subDomain/sermons` and `/sermons/:id`. Lists return `{data,pagination:{limit,offset,count}}` with default limit 50, maximum 100, and offset maximum 10,000. Responses project explicit public sermon fields and exclude church settings, drafts, and member records. `hidePublicSite` disables the feed. Cross-origin access uses the Worker's allowlist; responses are not cached so unpublishing takes effect immediately. No website page-building routes are implemented.

## R2 archive files

Files belong to an existing sermon in the authenticated church. POST `/api/content/files/upload?fileName=example.mp3&contentType=sermon&contentId=<sermon-id>` sends raw bytes with the MIME `Content-Type` header, up to 25 MiB. The upstream one-item base64 POST `/api/content/files` shape is also accepted, up to 5 MiB decoded: `[{"contentType":"sermon","contentId":"sermon-demo","fileName":"transcript.txt","fileType":"text/plain","fileContents":"data:text/plain;base64,SGVsbG8="}]`.

Uploads return an array with server-owned `id`, file metadata, `provider: "r2"`, and `contentPath`. GET `/files/sermon/:id` lists archive metadata; GET/HEAD `/files/download/:id` serves authenticated bytes and metadata; DELETE `/files/:id` removes the file. Filenames reject path separators and control characters. PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, PDF, plain text, MP3, WAV, MP4 audio/video, ZIP, and generic binary MIME types are accepted; SVG, HTML, and script MIME types are rejected. Bearer download routes return attachments with `nosniff`, a sandbox CSP, and `private, no-store, no-transform`. The separate native player route permits inline audio/video after the same live access checks; other file types remain download-only. Neither file DTOs nor upload sessions disclose R2 object keys, provider upload ids, or part ETags.

File ids are immutable. Replacing an archive means uploading a new file and deleting the old file. Existing archive files must be removed before deleting their sermon. Uploads persist `pending` metadata before writing bytes, then recheck permission and pending state while marking `ready` and auditing in a D1 batch. A lost response from that batch is reconciled before compensation, preserving confirmed-ready bytes. Deletes mark `deleting` before removing bytes, so failed operations stay hidden and recoverable. `cleanupFiles` atomically claims ordinary pending/deleting operations older than one hour before removing bytes, at most 100 per scheduled execution, while excluding active multipart sessions. Multipart cleanup independently aborts expired sessions and retries cancellation tombstones. This does not pretend R2 and D1 share a distributed transaction.

### Private range downloads

GET accepts one `Range: bytes=start-end`, `bytes=start-`, or `bytes=-suffix` field. Satisfiable ranges return 206, an exact `Content-Range`, and the selected byte count; bounds and oversized suffixes clamp to the object size. An unsatisfiable range returns 416 with `Content-Range: bytes */size`. Unknown units, malformed fields and multiple-range requests are ignored with an ordinary 200 response; multipart/byteranges responses are not implemented. HEAD ignores Range and reports the full representation length without sending bytes. The shared helper powers staff and member downloads; tenant, permission and current publication checks happen before object metadata or range errors are exposed.

Responses include a quoted R2 ETag, Last-Modified, and Accept-Ranges. `If-Range` accepts an exact strong ETag or exact Last-Modified date; mismatch returns the full representation. `If-None-Match` and `If-Modified-Since` can return 304 only after authorization. No token is accepted in a URL. CORS uses the installation allowlist and exposes only the necessary download headers. Identity-encoded responses preserve exact Content-Length; local Miniflare may automatically compress/chunk a full 200 text response, so full-length tests explicitly request identity encoding. Range and HEAD responses are checked against actual local runtime headers and bytes.

### Native private playback and downloads

The admin archive controls and member portal use native `<audio>`/`<video>` playback and browser downloads, without buffering the archive as an application Blob. Clients first POST `/api/media/access` with bearer authentication, JSON `{fileId,audience:"member"|"staff"}`, and an exact same-origin `Origin`. The response returns `{playUrl,downloadUrl,expiresAt}`. These stable URLs contain only file identifiers, never bearer or media credentials. `/api/media/files/:fileId/play` and `/download` accept GET/HEAD with the browser's scoped cookies; the existing bearer download endpoints remain available for suite/API clients.

Each grant uses 256 random bits from Web Crypto; D1 stores only its SHA-256 hash, bound to one file, church, user, login session, session revocation version, and member/staff audience. `__Secure-b1-media` is HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict, host-only and scoped to `/api/media/files/:fileId/`. Separate file paths allow concurrent archives. A second HttpOnly context cookie at `/api/media/` binds these file grants to the currently signed-in browser session. Successful account replacement changes that context, so an old account's still-live file cookie cannot serve media in the newly signed-in context. Duplicate matching cookies are rejected. Native routes never fall back to bearer authentication; cookies cannot authenticate ordinary APIs.

A grant expires after at most two hours, capped by its login session's expiry; the UI can refresh it by selecting Play or Download again. There are at most twenty active file grants per session. Issuance checks live authority, file/sermon state, the session revocation version and the grant limit in the same D1 batch that stores the grant. A race with revocation or content changes fails instead of creating a stale grant. Reissuing the same file replaces its prior grant. DELETE `/api/media/access` with bearer authentication and same-origin `Origin` revokes the session's grants and clears their cookies. Successful church selection and logout do the same; invalid church selection does not. Scheduled maintenance removes expired grants, and session/file deletion cascades their rows.

Every native GET, HEAD, conditional request and range rechecks login expiry, password version, account state, current church membership, grant scope, ready file state, and current sermon authority. Member-audience grants always require current public visibility, publication and a nonfuture publish date, including when issued by staff. Staff-audience grants use the current sermon/playlist resource permission. Unpublishing, deleting or revoking access blocks the next request before R2 reads or a possible 304. Already received browser buffers and an already-authorized in-flight response cannot be retracted; the portal clears its player on logout, token changes and unmount.

Cookie routes reject cross-origin and same-site sibling embedding, even origins allowed for bearer CORS. Responses use `Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy: same-origin` and private/no-store caching. Production media requires HTTPS. Local development/test permits loopback HTTP while retaining Secure cookies; the development Vite proxy accepts only its explicit loopback origin with same-origin fetch metadata. This is a same-origin browser feature; separate sites should use the documented bearer API through their own authenticated backend, not reuse these cookies cross-origin. The portal service worker does not cache API/media responses.

The implementation uses the browser's native codec support and does not transcode, package adaptive streams, ingest live media, or claim every browser/codec combination. See [MDN's cookie security guidance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Security/Practical_implementation_guides/Cookies), [Cloudflare's streaming response guidance](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/runtime-apis/streams/), and [R2 ranged reads](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/api/workers/workers-api-reference/). The upstream `FileController` uses authenticated download redirects for private provider files; this port keeps the stable file-locator concept but serves private R2 bytes directly instead of exposing provider URLs.

### Resumable R2 multipart uploads

The new `/api/content/files/uploads` API supports files from 1 byte through **1 GiB**, with **8 MiB** parts except the smaller final part. Raw uploads retain their 25 MiB limit and legacy base64 retains 5 MiB. Sessions last 24 hours, belong to the initiating user and church, and require Content Edit or StreamingServices Edit even for status reads. There can be three active sessions per user and twenty per church. These are application limits, not Cloudflare service maxima.

| Operation | Request and response |
| --- | --- |
| POST `/files/uploads` | JSON `{contentId,fileName,fileType,size,idempotencyKey}`; returns 201 for a new session, 200 for an identical replay. Use a random 16–100 character key. Reusing a key with different metadata returns 409. |
| GET `/files/uploads?contentId=:sermonId` | Own unfinished sessions, up to 20. |
| GET `/files/uploads/:id` | Session status and completed logical parts `{partNumber,size,sha256}`. |
| PUT `/files/uploads/:id/parts/:number` | Raw bytes with the exact expected part size; returns `{partNumber,size,sha256}`. SHA-256 is computed by the server. |
| POST `/files/uploads/:id/complete` | Freezes the server-owned manifest and completes R2; returns the complete session with its file DTO. Replays return the same file. |
| DELETE `/files/uploads/:id` | Aborts/discards an unfinished upload; repeated abort is safe. Completed files use the ordinary file-delete endpoint. |

A session DTO contains `id`, `fileId`, `contentId`, `fileName`, `fileType`, `size`, `partSize`, `totalParts`, `state`, `expiresAt`, and `parts`; completed sessions also contain `file`. States are creating, uploading, completing, complete, aborting and aborted. Expired sessions return 410 on part/completion writes. Uploaded part bytes are immutable: a retry with different bytes returns 409. Eight attempts per logical part are permitted before a new session is required.

Each attempt reserves a distinct physical R2 part number. The first successful D1 part selection wins, so a late retry cannot overwrite bytes selected for completion. Clients never supply the final ETag manifest. Completing requires all contiguous logical parts and the exact declared total size. The final object must match the session id, church id, file id and size in server-set R2 metadata before the pending file becomes ready. D1 finalization and audit are atomic; incomplete files are absent from staff lists, member lists and downloads. Existing publication gates remain in force, and the public sermon feed does not expose archive objects.

An unknown completion result stays in completing state with its manifest frozen. Retrying checks verified R2 metadata and can recover a successful object write after a lost response or D1 failure. A negative HEAD is never treated as proof that an in-flight write failed. Cancellation claims the D1 state before aborting R2 and deleting any completed bytes; finalization cannot publish after cancellation wins. Aborted sessions retain seven-day cleanup tombstones so later object arrivals are removed. Sessions whose R2 creation is known to have failed are marked aborted; a confirmed failure to persist the newly created upload id attempts immediate R2 abort. If both R2 creation and D1 persistence become uncertain, the provider's incomplete-upload expiry remains the final safety net; no pending object is published. [Cloudflare documents these multipart and consistency behaviors](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/api/workers/workers-api-reference/), including [the limits of negative reads during writes](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/consistency/#negative-reads-and-in-flight-writes).

The admin editor selects multipart automatically above 25 MiB and offers it for smaller files. It shows progress, pause, refresh, resume and abort. After reauthentication/reselection, every saved chunk's SHA-256 is checked before new chunks are uploaded, preventing accidental mixing of two same-name/same-size files. The native cookie-backed playback/download flow above removes the earlier 25 MiB browser Blob limit; raw upload remains capped at 25 MiB. Existing HTTPS media URLs may be saved as metadata; no live media is fetched or imported.

## Tests

`cd cloudflare && node --test test/calendar.test.mjs test/recurrence.test.mjs test/member.test.mjs test/sermons.test.mjs test/media.test.mjs test/media-access.test.mjs test/media-security.test.mjs` exercises the actual Worker with local D1/R2 bindings, covering CRUD, audit/atomicity, tenant isolation, viewer restrictions, civil dates, UTC offsets, DST gaps/folds/non-hour changes, recurrence bounds, exceptions, atomic occurrence changes and future splits, member calendar privacy, published-only sermon reads, base64 compatibility, R2 bytes, and file delete constraints. The browser recurrence test in `tests/cloudflare/calendar-recurrence.spec.ts` exercises creation, skip/restore, future editing, and series deletion through the UI against local bindings. The media suite verifies real local multipart bytes and restart persistence, concurrent/retried parts, uncertain completion, injected D1 failures, cancellation races, expiry, tenant/user/custom-role boundaries, range/HEAD validators and metadata privacy. Browser `tests/cloudflare/media-uploads.spec.ts` covers pause, reauthentication, original-file checks, resume, byte integrity and abort with synthetic data. `tests/cloudflare/media-playback.spec.ts` passed in installed Chrome against the compiled Worker/assets: a generated 28 MiB PCM WAV (29,360,172 bytes including its header) decoded, sought beyond byte 25 MiB through a native 206 range request, and downloaded with matching SHA-256. It also verified Secure/HttpOnly/Strict cookies on loopback, account replacement rejecting the previous media context, stale-context grant rejection, logout cookie clearing, and no bearer header on native requests. Playwright Secure-cookie inspection uses the full cookie jar because HTTP URL filtering omits Secure cookies even when Chromium sends them on trustworthy loopback. The configured 1 GiB limit is validated; backend restart/concurrency payloads are 8 MiB plus a small final part, and the largest browser-tested archive is the 28 MiB WAV plus its header across four parts; a full 1 GiB throughput benchmark was not run. The two upload tests, member portal test and original sermon workflow passed in the first focused run; the native playback test passed after correcting only Playwright cookie introspection. Other browsers, video codecs and long-duration throughput remain unverified. The local runtime needs permission to bind a loopback server in sandboxed environments.

## Plan-type/group associations

The original PlanTypeGroups panel uses GET `/api/membership/associatedGroups/content/planType/:id`, GET `/associatedGroups/group/:id?contentType=planType`, POST arrays to `/associatedGroups`, and DELETE `/associatedGroups/:id`. Records contain `id`, `churchId`, `contentType: "planType"`, `contentId`, `groupId`, and `settings: "past" | "future" | "both"`. Only this association kind is implemented. Writes require Plans Edit; reads require Plans View or Edit. D1 enforces both tenant-qualified foreign keys and unique plan-type/group pairs. Changes are audited atomically.

For published plans, an association grants group members, including members of descendant groups, access to the selected plan-date range: past is today or earlier, future is today or later, and both permits either, using the church-local date. Draft/prepared plans remain hidden; removing an association takes effect on the next read. Assignment-based access remains independent. Ancestor-group visibility and the original group-plans route are implemented; timeline plan-card generation remains unported. Permission scope inheritance is separate: a scoped administrative grant never expands to child groups.
